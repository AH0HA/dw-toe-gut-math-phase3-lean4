# PART II: The Bridge — From Mass to Gravity (v2)

## Chapter 5: The Bridge That Broke, and What It Taught Us

> "Eight dimensions of drag: a beautiful idea we measured to death, with
> full honors."

v1's Chapter 5 was the boldest claim in the book: a closed formula for
the gravitational coupling, α_G = 847δ⁸/(720π), where 847 = 7×121,
720 = 6×120, and the sevens and 120s came from genuine icosahedral
geometry. This chapter now tells the truth about that formula — first
what was real in it, then how it failed, then why keeping the corpse on
display makes the framework stronger.

**The real geometry [M].** The icosidodecahedron — the rectification of
icosahedron and dodecahedron — has at each vertex two triangles and two
pentagons: face angles summing to 336°, an angular deficit of 24°, and a
triangle-pentagon wedge of 168° standing to that deficit in the exact
ratio 7:1. All true, all checkable with a protractor, all worth teaching
(the *Golden Struts* kit builds this solid).

**The failure [M — audited].** Three independent cuts, any one fatal:

1. *The exponent lost its parent.* The 8 in δ⁸ was justified as "one
   drag factor per lost E₈ dimension" through the eight-factor theta
   conjecture — which is provably impossible (Chapter 29, Theorem 0).
   The icosian reformulation's natural multiplicities are 4 and 2.
2. *The formula fails its own accuracy claim.* With this chapter's own
   v1 value of δ it lands +116 ppm from the measured α_G — not the
   claimed ~50 ppm, and five times outside CODATA's stated uncertainty
   on G (22 ppm).
3. *The eighth power amplifies ambiguity into tuning.* v1's chapters
   carried slightly different δ values (a fourth-decimal spread); raised
   to the eighth power, that spread swings the "prediction" across a
   2,100 ppm range. A formula whose success depends on the fifth digit
   of an underived input is a fit wearing a derivation's clothes. The
   full sensitivity table is committed in
   `re-work-axiom-07162026/NUMERICAL_AUDIT.md`, Table 2 — run it.

**Why display the wreck [I].** Because this is what the Wright Clause
costs. The Wrights crashed gliders for three years and *logged every
crash*; the logs, not the luck, are why the fourth year flew. A
framework that quietly deletes its failed formulas trains its readers to
distrust its surviving ones. This one stays — labeled, dated, and
instructive: it is precisely how a numerological fit *feels* from the
inside before you audit it, and every reader who absorbs that lesson is
harder to fool, including by us.

**What a real bridge would need [M/P].** Parameter-free inputs (one δ,
stated to full precision, or none); exponents derived from the icosian
structure (4 and 2, not 8); and an error budget honestly compared to
G's own measurement spread *before* publication, not after.

**What This Lets Us Build.** A habit, not a device: the audit script as
standard equipment. Every quantitative claim this book makes from here
forward ships with its `audit.py` line number.
