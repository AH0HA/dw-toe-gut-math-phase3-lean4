## Reader's Caution, v2 (replaces the v1 caution)

The first edition of this book carried a caution: the entire framework
rested on one central, unproven conjecture. That caution did its job.
In July 2026 the conjecture — the eight-factor factorization of the E₈
theta function — was tested and **proven impossible** (the proof takes
two lines and is in Chapter 29). A framework that cannot lose cannot win;
this one could lose, did lose, and kept what survived. What survived was
substantial: the mathematics of icosahedral order turned out to run
*deeper* than the broken version claimed, through the icosian ring —
E₈ seen as four golden dimensions rather than eight ordinary ones — and
the framework's central equation now lives there. Every claim in this
edition is tagged with its register, and the tags are the contract
between author and reader:

- **[M]** — mathematics: proven, or an open conjecture explicitly
  labeled as such.
- **[P]** — physics: a falsifiable postulate, with its falsifiers named.
- **[I]** — interpretation: a declared way of reading the mathematics.
  The math is compatible with it; the math does not prove it.

Nothing in this book presents [I] as [M]. Where the first edition did,
this edition says so and fixes it.

## The Wright Clause

One conviction organizes this edition: *if the framework is true, it
should let us build things — and we do not need the completed formalism
before we start building.* The Wright brothers flew in 1903 on wind-tunnel
data and relentless iteration, decades before theoretical aerodynamics
matured; thermodynamics itself was reverse-engineered from steam engines
that already worked. In that spirit, most chapters end with a short
section — **What This Lets Us Build** — naming the device, experiment, or
kit the chapter's ideas imply. The wind tunnel's modern descendant is the
blinded, pre-registered test (see Chapter 30): it is how a hypothesis
earns the right to be called an engine.

## How to read this edition

As a mathematical audit that survived its own knife; as a map of
consciousness across scales, honestly labeled; and as a build manual —
from the first paper icosahedron to the first blinded φ-resonator run.
